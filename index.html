Cotton Mather LEAKED: Reverend's Private Letter Reveals His REAL Interrogation Playbook

He said no to torture.

In writing.

To a judge.

That's the side he wanted us to see. What he wants you to ignore is the rest of the letter, where he walks that same judge through how to break someone without laying a finger on them.

Sources confirm the letter – dated May 31 1692 – was sent from Boston Minister Cotton Mather to Judge John Richards , chief judge of the court of Oyer and Terminer. Mather was too sick to attend in person, which luckily for us, meant he had to put it all in writing instead.

In his supposed denouncement of torture, he calls it “un-English.” Not wrong. Not cruel. Just uncivilized—--a clever appeal to who Richards thought he was.

But keep reading.

Buried, a few paragraphs later, Mather gets to his real advice.

Guilt.

Confusion.

Repetition.

“put the witches into confusion is likely to bring them unto confession too,” he writes. Translation: rattle them enough, they’ll say anything.

He even knows that the accumulation of guilt, and stress make them confess on their own. What he reads as, God himself “thunder-striking their souls.”

Mather’s personal favorite was having the accused recite the Lord’s Prayer. Idea was that a witch’s bond with the devil would cause them to stumble over the words. So the formula:

Mess it up → guilty.

Nervous → guilty.

Scared because you’re on trial for your life → somehow, guilty.

Now we here at WitchWatch™ are not saying witches aren’t a problem. Nobody’s saying that. We want them caught.

But here’s what no one is telling you: that “confession that they pulled out of your neighbor? Your fellow parishioners? Your kid? It might not mean what we all think it means. Mather’s own letter admits it—the goal isn’t finding guilty people, their methods will find people to say they are guilty, one way or another. And once the court confirms the confession, nobody’s asking how it got there.

Here’s what should worry you: Mather isn’t a random guy on the street corner yelling about Satan. He is one of our most respected ministers. He is educated, respected, The kind of person we just…believe.

That is exactly why this worked.

When a man like this puts on a façade of reasonability, nobody asks for receipts, nobody double checks. His reputation was the evidence.

Next time someone says a confession is "voluntary," ask about who decided that.

Ask what happens if your name is next on the list.

Court is still in session, sources say more letters are coming.
